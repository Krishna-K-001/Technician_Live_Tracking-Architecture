Live Technician Tracking - Filed Service

Giving every customer a real-time view of their arriving service technician — live location, estimated arrival time and turn-by-turn navigation — through a secure, branded web page that works on any device, with no app to install.

Platform: Salesforce Field Service · Experience Cloud  |  Audience: Global stakeholders  |  Version 1.0

1Executive summary

Customers waiting for a field service visit typically receive a broad time window and no visibility of where the technician is. This creates uncertainty, repeated “where is my technician?” calls, and sites that are not ready when the technician arrives.

TechLive solves this by publishing a secure tracking page for each Service Appointment. While the technician is travelling, the customer sees the technician's live position on a map, an estimated time of arrival, the confirmed destination and a one-tap link to live navigation. When the technician arrives, tracking switches off automatically.

Real-time

Position refreshes every 10 seconds from the technician's mobile GPS.

Zero install

Runs in any browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Low cost

No paid map API keys, no external callouts, built on existing licences.

Privacy-first

Location is shared only during travel and only when tracking is enabled.

2The problem

Consumers now expect the transparency they get from ride-hailing and parcel delivery. Field service visits — often for business-critical equipment — have not kept pace.

Uncertain arrival windows

Customers are given multi-hour windows and must keep staff and equipment on standby without knowing when the technician will actually arrive.

“Where is my technician?” calls

Customers call the service desk or dispatcher for updates, consuming time that could be spent planning and supporting work.

Site not ready on arrival

Without advance notice, site access, safety permits, contact persons or equipment shutdowns are not prepared — causing waiting time, failed visits and reschedules.

Technician distraction

Technicians phone ahead or answer calls while driving to give status updates, which is unsafe and unproductive.

Wrong destination pin

Stored coordinates on records can be imprecise, sending navigation to the wrong building or entrance on large industrial sites.

Inconsistent experience

Status updates vary by region, dispatcher and technician, so customers worldwide receive a different level of service.

Problem statement: Customers lack real-time, trustworthy visibility of an arriving technician, which drives avoidable contact volume, wasted on-site time and lower customer satisfaction.

3The solution

A customer-facing, branded tracking page hosted on a Salesforce Experience Cloud site, driven entirely by data that Field Service already captures.

Live technician map

Embedded map showing the route from the technician's current position to the customer site.

Estimated arrival

Plain-language ETA such as “Technician will arrive in about 18 min”, recalculated on every refresh.

Confirmed destination

Customer name and service address shown, so the customer can confirm the correct site.

One-tap navigation

“Open navigation in Google Maps” for a live, traffic-aware ETA and route.

Freshness indicator

“Technician location updated: <time>” so customers know how current the data is.

Automatic end of tracking

When travel ends, the page shows “Technician Has Arrived” and stops sharing location.

4Customer journey

  1. Schedule. The dispatcher schedules the Service Appointment and assigns a technician in Salesforce Field Service.
  2. Start travel. The technician sets the appointment to Travel in the Field Service mobile app. Tracking is enabled for the appointment (MT_Tracking_Active__c).
  3. Share link. The customer receives the tracking link for their appointment (e.g., by email or SMS through the organisation's notification process).
  4. Track. The customer opens the link on any device and sees the live map, ETA, destination and last-update time. The page refreshes automatically every 10 seconds.
  5. Prepare. As the technician approaches, the customer arranges site access, contacts and equipment.
  6. Arrive. When the appointment leaves the Travel status or tracking is switched off, the page shows “Technician Has Arrived” and location sharing stops.

What the customer sees

LocatingTracking is active but no technician GPS position is available yetSpinner — “Locating your technician…”

6Detailed design

6.1 Service contract — getTechnicianLocation(saId)

A single Apex method returns everything the page needs. Data is only returned when the appointment is in Travel status and MT_Tracking_Active__c is true.

FieldDescription
activefalse when the appointment is not travelling or tracking is off — nothing else is returned
locationAvailableWhether the assigned technician has a last-known GPS position
latitude, longitudeTechnician's last-known position (ServiceResource)
asOfTimestamp of the technician's last GPS update
customerLatitude, customerLongitudeService site coordinates (Work Order, falling back to Service Appointment)
customerAddressFormatted address (street, city, state, postal code, country)
customerNameAccount name of the customer site

6.2 Customer location resolution

  1. The Account holds the authoritative local geocode (MT_Local_Latitude__c, MT_Local_Longitude__c).
  2. Before-save flows copy this geocode onto every Work Order and Service Appointment.
  3. The controller prefers Work Order coordinates, address and account name, and falls back to the Service Appointment.
  4. The map destination is sent as “Account Name, Address”, so Google Maps performs a place search and pins the actual business; coordinates are used only if no address exists.

6.3 ETA estimation (no paid API)

The on-page ETA is a lightweight estimate computed in the browser:

distance_km = haversine(technician, customer) × 1.3   // road factor
eta_minutes = round(distance_km ÷ 30 km/h × 60)   // average urban speed

Results ≤ 1 minute display “Technician is arriving now”; longer journeys show hours and minutes. The page labels this as approximate and offers the Google Maps link for a live, traffic-aware ETA. Both the speed and road factor are tunable parameters.

6.4 Polling and lifecycle

  • Polling starts when the page reads the appointment ID (id or c__id URL parameter) and repeats every 10 seconds.
  • Polling stops when tracking ends, when an error occurs, or when the customer leaves the page — no background load is left behind.

6.5 Branding

The page carries a corporate brand header and an auto-scrolling carousel of product lines (Precision, Laboratory, Industrial, Retail). Images are packaged as Salesforce static resources, so the page has no dependency on external image hosting.

8Security & privacy

Data minimisation

Only the technician's position and timestamp, plus the customer's own site name and address, are returned. No technician personal details, contact data or work content are exposed.

Time-bound sharing

Location is returned only while the appointment is in Travel and tracking is explicitly enabled. After arrival, the page receives nothing.

Read-only access

The page performs no updates; the controller only reads a fixed set of fields.

No third-party data transfer via API

No server-side callouts. The customer's browser loads the map directly from Google using the coordinates and address shown on the page.

Recommendations before global rollout:
  • Replace the raw Service Appointment ID in the link with a signed, expiring, non-guessable token.
  • Confirm technician location-sharing consent and works-council / labour-law requirements per country.
  • Review the privacy notice and data processing terms for map-provider use (e.g., GDPR, CCPA, PIPL, LGPD).
  • Configure Experience Cloud guest-user profile with the minimum Apex class access only.

12Rollout approach

Phase 1 — Pilot

One country / service team. Capture KPI baseline, validate GPS freshness and ETA accuracy, gather customer feedback.

Phase 2 — Regional

Add secure tokens, automated notifications and translations. Complete privacy and works-council reviews per country.

Phase 3 — Global

Region-specific ETA tuning and map providers, KPI dashboards, and standard operating procedures for all service organisations.

Prerequisites per country

  • Field Service mobile app location tracking enabled for technicians.
  • Account local geocodes and addresses maintained.
  • Experience Cloud site published with guest access to the tracking page.
  • Process defined for enabling tracking and sending the link to the customer.
